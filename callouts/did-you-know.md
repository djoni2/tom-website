## New additions

### Madeira's cholera epidemic killed thousands in 1856

> **Did you know?** The cholera epidemic that reached Madeira in July 1856 killed thousands of people. Regional historical records describe a particularly devastating period at the end of July, when hundreds of bodies were buried at the Angústias cemetery within two days.

- Topics: cholera, public health, Funchal history
- Source: https://drabl.madeira.gov.pt/os-nossos-recursos/propostas-tematicas/epidemias-na-madeira
- Status: Verify the total death toll and cemetery figures against the original archive record.

### Madeira's embroidery industry had more than 1,000 workers in 1862

> **Did you know?** Historical industrial statistics recorded 1,029 embroiderers in Madeira in 1862. The trade was already generating significant sales, although the women doing the work received very low daily wages.

- Topics: embroidery, women's work, economic history
- Source: https://elucidariomadeirense.pt/en/articles/538

### Madeira's embroidery exports collapsed in just two years

> **Did you know?** A historical account puts the value of Madeira's embroidery exports at 55,252,000 réis in 1878, falling to 12,937,000 réis by 1880. The decline was linked partly to changing fashion in England, showing how distant markets could affect Madeiran households.

- Topics: embroidery, exports, economic history
- Source: https://elucidariomadeirense.pt/en/articles/538
- Status: Verify the original figures, currency notation and explanation for the decline.

### German merchants helped Madeira embroidery reach new markets

> **Did you know?** German embroidery export houses began operating in Funchal around 1890. Historical accounts describe the trade expanding beyond its earlier British market to include customers in Germany, the United States and elsewhere.

## The forgotten infrastructure of Funchal

### Funchal once had a mountain railway, not just a cable car

> **Did you know?** The Monte railway opened its first section in July 1893 and eventually reached Terreiro da Luta in 1912. The line climbed from Funchal into the hills above the city before closing in 1943. Today's cable car is a separate system and does not follow the old railway route.

- Topics: Monte, transport history, Funchal
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Railway

### An explosion interrupted Funchal's mountain railway in 1919

> **Did you know?** The Monte railway's history includes an explosion in October 1919, reportedly between Levada de Santa Luzia and Livramento. The line reopened in February 1920, according to a published historical timeline.

- Topics: Monte railway, accidents, transport history

### Funchal's railway was built in stages over nearly 20 years

> **Did you know?** The Monte railway did not reach its final upper terminus in one construction phase. Its first section opened in 1893, another followed in 1894, and the extension to Terreiro da Luta was inaugurated in 1912.

## Porto Santo's less familiar history

### European maps showed Porto Santo before Portuguese settlement

> **Did you know?** Historical accounts point to medieval nautical charts that depicted Porto Santo before the Portuguese settlement process, including the Angelino Dulcert portolan of around 1339 and the Mediceo-Laurenziano portolan of 1351. These maps raise questions about how much European maritime knowledge of the archipelago predated its official discovery narrative.

- Topics: Porto Santo, cartography, maritime history
- Source: Casa Colombo / Porto Santo museum; verify the museum's historical account and the interpretation of the maps.

### Columbus's Porto Santo house comes with a historical caveat

> **Did you know?** Porto Santo's Casa Colombo is linked by local tradition to Christopher Columbus, who married Filipa Moniz, daughter of the island's captain-donee. However, the association relies partly on historical tradition, and the surviving architectural features do not prove that every part of the present house dates from his lifetime.

- Topics: Columbus, Porto Santo, heritage
- Source: Casa Colombo / Porto Santo museum; verify the museum's current interpretation before publication.

### Porto Santo's rabbits attracted scientific attention

> **Did you know?** Porto Santo's rabbits attracted scientific attention in the 19th century because they appeared different from British rabbits. A 1921 letter in the journal Nature revisited the question, arguing that the animals belonged to a wider southern European rabbit group rather than representing a unique evolutionary discovery.

- Topics: Porto Santo, natural history, scientific history
- Source: https://www.nature.com/ (locate the original 1921 letter before publication).

### Porto Santo has a legend about a peak supposedly given by a king

> **Did you know?** A local story about Pico de Ana Ferreira claims that a woman connected to King João II was given the peak as a possession. When she complained that it was useful only for grazing animals, she was told the land also included fields that depended on rainfall.

- Topics: Porto Santo, Pico de Ana Ferreira, folklore
- Source: https://portosantobiosfera.madeira.gov.pt/en/cultural-heritage/intangible-heritage/legends-and-stories.html
- Status: Local legend; do not present the royal connection as established fact.

> **Did you know?** Madeira's historical records include a plague outbreak spanning 1521 to 1538, a major cholera epidemic in 1856, bubonic plague in 1905 and another cholera outbreak in 1910. Together, these episodes show that the island's public-health history extends well beyond the better-known 19th-century crisis.

- Topics: epidemics, public health, social history
