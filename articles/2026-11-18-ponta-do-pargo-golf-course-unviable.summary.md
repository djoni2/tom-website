# Study Finds Ponta do Pargo Golf Course Unviable

A feasibility study commissioned by Madeira's regional government concludes that the golf course under construction at **Ponta do Pargo**, in the Calheta municipality, is not financially viable as a public project. Opposition lawmakers from the Socialist Party (PS) revealed the finding this week.

The disclosure comes as construction on the course moves into an **advanced stage**, and as a public tender for the surrounding real estate land moves toward its deadline with a lower starting price than originally planned.

PS lawmakers said they found the price cut odd. They asked the government which studies and partners backed the decision. In response, they were sent the project's own **feasibility study** - and found that the study itself states the golf course, as a public investment, is not financially viable.

> The study itself indicates that the golf course, as a public project, is financially unviable.

**PS lawmaker**

The regional government does not dispute the finding. It argues that no golf course anywhere in the world is **financially self-sustaining** on its own.

Such projects, officials say, always need to be **paired with hotel** or real estate investment, as is the case at Ponta do Pargo. A golf course cannot sustain itself on green-fee sales alone.

## Tender Price For Golf Concession Falls to €16 Million

The public tender covers both the concession to run the golf course and the sale of land earmarked for real estate around it. Its base price was cut from 18 million euros to **16 million euros**, payable in four installments, with a large discount against the concession's original value.

A separate, smaller error also affected the tender when it was launched. The original land valuation covered more land than is actually being sold, and the government says the tender has since been **corrected to match** the area that will actually change hands.

## Public Developer Has Invested About €60 Million So Far

The golf course and its surrounding land belong to **Ponta do Oeste**, the public company that has developed the site. PS lawmakers say the company has invested about **60 million euros** in the project so far, most of it on buying the land. That process began roughly 20 years ago and is ending this year as planned.

PS lawmakers call the resulting **loss very large**, comparing its scale to the cost of building a new marina at Lugar de Baixo.

Because the golf course itself is **run as a concession** rather than sold outright, that part of the land stays with the public company. The separate real-estate parcel, earmarked for the villas and hotels, is the one actually being sold.

## Plan Calls For Two Hotels Alongside New Villas

The wider project foresees **160 villas** and two hotels built around the golf course. PS lawmakers say the feasibility study's main economic benefit actually comes from building those villas in Calheta, not from the golf course itself.

They argue that building villas in Calheta does not require public investment, since the area already has **no shortage** of housing for sale. The study also prices the villas' land below the current market value, the lawmakers say.

## Construction Alone Seen Adding €160 Million in Wealth

Despite finding the golf course financially unviable on its own, the feasibility study rates the wider project as economically viable - highly viable, officials say - across **three different scenarios**. Much of that wider benefit comes from construction activity.

Officials estimate construction work alone, covering the golf course, the hotels and the real estate, will generate about **160 million euros** in wealth. PS lawmakers argue the government will not recover anywhere close to what it has spent on the golf course itself, even counting that wider economic activity.

## Golf Course Due to Open in First Half of 2027

The regional government expects the golf course to be completed in the **first half of 2027**. PS lawmakers say they will keep monitoring the project's costs and outcomes.

## Investor Tender Closes This Friday, November 20

The public tender for the concession and land sale initially drew **no interested investors**, forcing the government to extend it. Officials then lowered both the price and the conditions attached to the deal.

The revised tender is now scheduled to close on **20 November**, this Friday. The government says the winning bidders for the real estate and golf course operation will be known within a month. PS lawmakers argue that the lack of early private-sector interest is itself a sign of the project's weak financial and economic viability.
