# Four Tuberculosis Cases Identified in Câmara de Lobos

Health authorities in Madeira have identified **four cases of tuberculosis** in a residential area in the municipality of Câmara de Lobos.

The regional health service, **SESARAM**, announced the cases in a statement, the newspaper DNOTICIAS reported.

## Cases Identified on 3 November in One Residential Area

According to SESARAM, the cases were identified **on Tuesday, 3 November**. The statement does not say which residential area is affected.

## Two Health Services Isolate the Patients and Investigate

The situation is being followed by the **Public Health Unit** and the Câmara de Lobos Health Centre.

They have put in place **prevention and control measures**. These include isolating the cases, clinical monitoring of the patients and an epidemiological investigation.

The local health centre has also made **rooms available** for screening tests and for treating the patients who were identified.

## SESARAM Activates Its Contingency Plan After the Câmara de Lobos Cases

At the same time, SESARAM has **activated its contingency plan** and stepped up its prevention measures.

According to the health service, its **services are not affected** for now:

> "So far, services are operating normally, with no impact on the care provided to patients."

**SESARAM**, Madeira's regional health service, in a statement

SESARAM reminded the public that **strict compliance** with prevention and control measures is essential. It said this is key to limiting the spread of this infectious disease and protecting the health of the whole community.

## WHO Says 5 to 10% of Infected People Develop the Disease

Tuberculosis is an **infectious disease** caused by bacteria. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), it most often affects the lungs.

It spreads **through the air** when people with TB cough, sneeze or spit. The WHO lists a prolonged cough, chest pain, weakness, tiredness, weight loss, fever and night sweats as common symptoms.

Only about **5 to 10%** of people infected with TB eventually develop symptoms and the disease, the WHO says. It also stresses that tuberculosis is preventable and curable.
