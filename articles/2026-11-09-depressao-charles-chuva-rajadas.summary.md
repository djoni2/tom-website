# Depression Charles to Bring 130 Km/h Gusts to Madeira

A depression named **Charles** is moving toward Madeira. The Portuguese weather institute, **IPMA**, expects a significant worsening of conditions.

The worst weather is forecast for **Tuesday night into Wednesday**, November 11. IPMA expects persistent rain, strong wind gusts and rougher seas, especially off the south coast.

## IPMA Forecasts Over 100 Millimeters of Rain in 12 Hours

Rainfall could **exceed 100 millimeters** in just 12 hours in some parts of the region. The worst-hit areas will be the mountains.

The wind will blow from the southwest. Gusts should reach **80 to 100 km/h** along the coasts, and up to 130 km/h in high ground.

Sea conditions will also worsen. IPMA forecasts southwest waves of **3.5 to 4.5 meters** on the south coast.

## Orange Warnings Issued, With Possible Worsening Ahead

Faced with this forecast, IPMA has announced **orange-level weather warnings** for Madeira. The institute has not ruled out a **further worsening** in some areas, depending on updates to its forecast models.

## Depression Centered at 994 Hectopascals, Moving East

Charles currently sits at 33 degrees north and 31 degrees west. Its **central pressure** is 994 hectopascals, and it is moving eastward.

By 00:00 UTC on Wednesday, forecasts place the center of the depression **north of Madeira**. It should then continue toward mainland Portugal, though IPMA stresses there is **high uncertainty** over its exact path and strength.

## Mainland Portugal Faces Gusts of Up to 120 Km/h on Wednesday

On the mainland, the effects of the depression should begin from the **end of Wednesday morning**. Rain could be heavy at times, with gusts of **100 to 120 km/h**, or even higher in some places.

The most likely track currently points to the depression's center passing between **Sines and Cabo Carvoeiro**.

## Separate System Bruna Stays Put Until Friday, Day 13

IPMA has clarified that **depression Bruna**, which is expected to remain largely stationary northwest of the Iberian Peninsula until Friday, November 13, is an **independent system** from Charles. The Azores archipelago is not expected to be affected by Charles.

The institute is advising residents to **follow upcoming updates**, since both the forecasts and the warning levels could still change.
