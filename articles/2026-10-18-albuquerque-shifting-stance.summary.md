# Albuquerque's Shifting Stance on Politicians Under Investigation

By **Mat**

The regional government president defended deputy Filipe Sousa, calling the investigation into him "baboseirada" and "hipocrisia". 
His past statements about António Costa and Pedro Calado reveal he has not applied a **consistent standard** to politicians under investigation.

## Shifting Standards Over Time

In November 2023, when **[António Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Costa)** resigned as prime minister following Operation Influencer - which involved a minister being named arguido and the detention of his chief of staff - **[Miguel Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Albuquerque)** stated it was "**very difficult**" for Costa to continue governing, despite Costa himself not being arguido. In January 2024, when Albuquerque himself was made arguido in an investigation involving suspected corruption and other crimes, which included his former vice-president **[Pedro Calado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Calado)** and two businessmen from the construction sector, he told journalists he had "**no problem**" being arguido and would not resign. By July 2024, Albuquerque refined his position, saying he would resign if accused but not merely for being arguido, stating that being arguido allowed him to know the facts and exercise his defence rights.

In the current case of Filipe Sousa (October 2026), Albuquerque declared he had "**no doubts**" about Sousa's honesty and dismissed the investigation as "baboseirada", noting that Sousa contested the allegations and claimed to have paid the bill in full for the alleged car rental benefit.

> "I think this is nonsense, a hypocrisy. Accusing everyone of everything is hypocritical. This is how they'll ruin politics."

**Miguel Albuquerque**, President of the Regional Government
(He said this on October 16)

The fact check concludes that claiming Albuquerque has **maintained the same standard** throughout is inaccurate. 

It notes that while he consistently defended the **presumption of innocence**, his assessment of whether politicians should remain in office during investigations varied depending on the circumstances and timing.
