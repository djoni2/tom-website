**The chamber of commerce** asked Funchal's city council to drop a plan to double the tourism tax from two euros to four euros. The trade association says the increase is unjustified when visitor numbers are slowing.

The ACIF-CCIM submitted its response during the public consultation on the proposal. The council has not shown the need for higher costs to visiting Funchal, the group argues, and raising charges now will unfairly hurt tourism while the economy remains uncertain.

## Tax Would Nearly Double to Four Euros

Funchal's city council wants to raise the **tourism tax from two to four euros** per visitor. The tax already exists. The increase would add a significant extra charge for anyone arriving in the city.

## Half the New Revenue Would Discourage Tourism

Of the four euros, **2.48 euros goes expressly to discourage visits**. On arrivals by ship, this component rises to 3.51 euros per passenger, making up the bulk of the total tax in both cases.

The association questions why the council would build a penalty into its own tax. Discouraging visitors conflicts with the goal of supporting tourism and the businesses that depend on it.

## Funchal Saw Only 0.2% Growth Last Quarter

Visitor demand is already weakening. In the second quarter of 2026, **overnight stays in Funchal grew just 0.2%** compared to the same period a year earlier. Stays by foreign residents fell by 1.4%.

The slowdown is visible across the region too. **Hotel occupancy fell to 78.9%** from 83.1%, a drop of 4.2 percentage points. Higher taxes at this moment will make the situation worse, ACIF says.

## Rising Fuel Costs May Worsen the Decline

**Fuel prices remain high**, making flights to Madeira more expensive. The region depends almost entirely on air travel, so any rise in airline costs cuts into visitor numbers.

The council should measure the total cost of a trip before adding new charges for the city, ACIF argues. Without lower fares or better value, more visitors will skip Madeira altogether.

## ACIF Wants Oversight and Spending Reports

The association demands **the creation of a consultative council** with public and private sector representatives to oversee how the money is used. The council would propose investment priorities, review the annual spending plan, and track projects.

The city should also **publish an annual plan** showing how much money was collected, which projects were funded, the costs and benefits of each, and all council opinions. Residents, businesses and visitors deserve to know where their tax money goes.

For ship arrivals, ACIF also wants **maritime agents to collect directly from ship owners** and pass only what they actually receive to the city, without going through the port authority APRAM. This change would spare agents from having to advance their own money or cover interest costs on delayed payments.
