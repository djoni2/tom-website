# Pedro Calado Wants to Return to Politics, But Not Against Albuquerque

Pedro Calado was once seen as the natural successor to **[Miguel Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Albuquerque)**. Albuquerque is president of the Regional Government of Madeira and leader of the regional PSD. In January 2024, a corruption investigation that led to his detention seemed to end that path.

Now the former mayor of Funchal says he wants to **return to politics**. He does not rule out leading the party, but he will not run against Albuquerque. Calado is the guest of 'Rostos da Autonomia' (Faces of Autonomy), a podcast by the newspaper DIÁRIO with the support of the Legislative Assembly of Madeira. It was released on 1 November 2026 on dnoticias.pt and TSF-Madeira.

## Born in Lisbon in 1971, Calado Spent His First Years in Mozambique

Calado was born in Lisbon in **October 1971** and has a twin brother, Vítor. When he was three months old, the family moved to Mozambique. His mother worked at the military mess there, and his father, a Madeiran, was a medical sales representative.

His mother is Angolan. In early 1974, sensing that political change was coming, his parents left for Angola. They then returned to Madeira **shortly before** the revolution of 25 April 1974. Other relatives were less fortunate.

> "With the revolution, many Portuguese lost what they had. That is exactly what happened to my uncles and maternal grandparents. They came with only the clothes they were wearing."

**Pedro Calado**, former Mayor of Funchal

## From KPMG in Lisbon to Madeira in 1998, Then Five Years in Banking

Calado lived in Madeira **until 1989**. He then moved to the mainland in the first year of Portugal's general university entrance exam. He studied management in Santarém and finished his degree at NOVA University Lisbon in 1994.

"Times were not easy and, with **two children** studying on the mainland, we went through some difficulties. I had a grant, and that is why I felt the need to work while I studied," he said.

After graduating, he joined the audit firm **KPMG** in Lisbon. He was later asked to open the firm's office in Madeira and returned to the island in 1998. He went on to spend five years in banking and worked with companies on financial matters.

## Albuquerque Brought Him Into Funchal City Council in 2005

Calado was a PSD sympathiser and had joined the party's youth wing, but he had **no political career**. That changed in 2005, when Albuquerque, then mayor of Funchal, invited him to join a new list for the city council.

He served the full 2005-2009 term and was **re-elected** with the PSD's absolute majority for the next one. Those years saw a split in the PSD between Albuquerque and [Alberto João Jardim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Jo%C3%A3o_Jardim), the [long-time head](https://www.timesofmadeira.com/king-of-madeira-alberto-joao-jardims-37-year-legacy-of-power) of the regional government. In 2012, Albuquerque lost a leadership vote by a few dozen votes, with Calado **at his side**. After the PSD's heavy losses in the 2013 local elections, Albuquerque won the party in 2014 and the regional election in 2015.

In the interview, Calado said the PSD had **not planned** properly for a successor in the Jardim years. In his view, this led to internal division and the loss of key municipalities in 2013. Parties, he argued, should plan leadership changes the way large companies do.

## Vice-President of the Regional Government From 2017, Mayor of Funchal in 2021

Calado did not join the regional government in 2015. He entered **halfway through** the term, in 2017, as vice-president. His task was to organise the executive. According to DIÁRIO, that work may have helped the PSD win the 2019 regional election against the Socialist Party (PS) of Paulo Cafôfo, who was seen as the favourite.

In 2021, he led the PSD list for **Funchal City Council** and won back the region's largest municipality, which the PS had run since 2013.

## Detained on 24 January 2024 and Held for About Three Weeks

On **24 January 2024**, while still mayor, Calado was detained in a police investigation into suspected corruption. He was held in Lisbon for about three weeks before a judge released him with the mildest measure available, a statement of identity and residence. He resigned as mayor while in custody.

In the podcast, Calado says he stayed **calm** because he knew nothing illegal had happened. He does not criticise the Public Prosecutor's Office for looking into complaints. He does criticise the way the operation was carried out, which he describes as a media show. He says he would have handed over documents from the first day if he had simply been asked.

He also says he might **not have resigned** had he known how the case would develop. He made that decision, he explains, to protect the council from constant media pressure.

## Nearly Three Years Later, Calado Says the Complaints Were Political

According to DIÁRIO, almost three years later the case **has not advanced**. Calado says the investigation had only political aims and that none of the suspicions have any basis. In his view, the complaints came from political opponents who wanted to weaken the region.

The case was followed by a period of **political instability**. Albuquerque resigned as head of the regional government on 26 January 2024, and Madeira held snap regional elections in May 2024 and March 2025. Calado argues that voters kept backing the PSD because they trusted the people who built the region's autonomy.

## Calado Proposes a 5% Threshold to Keep Small Parties Out of Parliament

Calado is critical of **small parties** polling at 1-3%. He accuses them of using minor issues and court injunctions. In his view, they block projects approved by large majorities. He warns that an excess of democracy has turned into a lack of restraint, where individuals and small movements can attack institutions without being held to account.

His answer is an **electoral threshold**. Parties that fail to reach 5% of the vote, as in Germany, should not have seats in legislative assemblies, he said. He also argued that four-year municipal terms are too short to complete major projects.

## Minimum Wage Doubled in 11 Years, Yet Young People Still Struggle

Calado also explained his recent comment asking where the **wealth has gone**. He said Madeira's economy has grown for more than 60 months in a row and that public debt has fallen to around 60-64% of GDP.

He listed other gains. The minimum wage has **doubled** in 11 years to almost 1,000 euros, and hospitals, water and electricity services have improved. Even so, he said, ordinary people and young professionals face high living costs and struggle to find housing. Politicians cannot ignore this, he argued, and must stop qualified young people from **leaving Madeira**.

## Calado Rules Out a Leadership Bid While Albuquerque, PSD Leader Since 2014, Stays

Calado says his whole political career has been built **alongside Albuquerque**. As long as Albuquerque leads the PSD, he will not run for the leadership and will stay by his side. He recalled that in 2015, when he disagreed with the make-up of the government, he chose to step back quietly instead of going public.

His family has urged him to stay away from politics. But Calado says he still has a **passion** for public service and is available to help shape Madeira's future when and where it makes sense.
