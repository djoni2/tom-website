# Albuquerque Defends Fee Hikes: 'Don't Come to Madeira'

Miguel Albuquerque, president of Madeira's Regional Government, defended the planned increase in **hiking trail fees** on Tuesday. He said visitors who object to the cost should stay away from the island.

Albuquerque spoke on the sidelines of a visit to the **Escola Secundária Francisco Franco**. He said the higher fees are justified by the cost of maintaining, cleaning and securing the island's levadas and trails.

> Hiking trails are a user-pays situation. You are using an infrastructure, and you are paying for using that infrastructure.
**Miguel Albuquerque**, President of Madeira's Regional Government

## Trail Fee for Pico Ruivo Set to Triple to €15

Asked about prices that **double or triple** for some routes, Albuquerque said the costlier trails would still carry a reasonable value. He compared Madeira's fees with those charged at other tourist destinations, without naming them.

The fee changes were announced by the regional government on 10 November. They raise the general trail fee for non-resident hikers from €4.50 to **€7.50**, effective 1 April 2027.

Four of the busiest routes will cost €15, **more than three times** today's price. They include the Vereda do Pico Ruivo and the Vereda da Ponta de São Lourenço.

## Areeiro's Full Route Climbs to €18

Albuquerque argued the experience justifies the cost on the island's **signature routes**.

> A person who visits Areeiro or Pico Ruivo has a unique experience, and has to pay for that experience. And if you don't want to pay, don't come here to Madeira.
**Miguel Albuquerque**

The Vereda do Areeiro, which covers the paths linked to Pico Ruivo and Encumeada, rises from €10.50 to €18. The **same fee** will cover the Rabaçal network, a cluster of trails that today charges separately for each route.

## Albuquerque Keeps Port Fee Flat at €2

Albuquerque said the regional government will **not raise** the tourist fee charged at the region's ports. He said it will stay at today's values.

That fee is charged per passenger disembarking at the **Port of Funchal** or in Porto Santo. It has stood at €2 since it was introduced.

On municipal tourist taxes, Albuquerque noted these fall under **each council's own authority**, not the regional government's.

## Business Group Has Warned of a 40% Demand Drop

Albuquerque's remarks come as the region's main business association, ACIF-CCIM, keeps pressing the government to reconsider. ACIF has forecast a demand drop of **up to 40%** for 2027, pointing to the fee increases and a wider global slowdown.

The regional secretary for Tourism, Eduardo Jesus, has already **ruled out backing down** on the fees, calling ACIF's forecast "alarmism." Albuquerque's comments add the regional president's own voice to that refusal.
