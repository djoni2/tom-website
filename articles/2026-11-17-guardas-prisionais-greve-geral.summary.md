# Portugal's Prison Guards Launch Nationwide Strike

Portugal's prison guards began a nationwide strike on November 17 that will run until **January 3, 2027**. The walkout was called by the Sindicato Nacional do Corpo da Guarda Prisional (SNCGP), the national union for the country's prison guard corps.

Minimum services and urgent work by prisons' **intervention and K9 units** continue through the strike. Every other prison guard duty is suspended for its duration.

The union says the strike is meant to force the government to honor commitments it had already negotiated. Those include **overtime pay rules**, a multi-year promotion plan and a second training course for prison chiefs, among other demands.

## Strike Runs From November 17 Until January 3

The walkout began today and will run until January 3, 2027. The union describes it as a general strike lasting about a **month and a half**.

## At Least 70% of Inmates Confined 22 Hours Daily

SNCGP president Frederico Morais told the Lusa news agency that at least 70% of inmates will be locked in their cells during the strike. The confinement will last **22 hours a day**.

Only indispensable procedures for inmates are exempt, along with **security operations** by the Prison Intervention and Security Group (GISP) and the Cynotechnical Operational Group (GOC).

## Court Ruling Limits Visits and Canteen to Once a Week

Under a ruling by the Lisbon Court of Appeal, prisons will not allow intimate visits or bags of food and clothing during the strike. Inmates will get only **one visit a week**.

The canteen will open **once a week** so inmates can buy necessities such as hygiene products and food.

Exceptions remain for escorts to **urgent medical appointments** when an inmate's health is at risk. Legal procedures involving an inmate's release or a change to their coercive measures are also exempt.

## Union Issued Strike Warning Notice on November 12

Morais said the minimum services ordered for the strike are guaranteed. They will be "enforced strictly," Morais said. In a notice sent to members on **November 12**, the union set out how it would respond to any violation.

> Any violation of the minimum services will be the subject of a formal and immediate complaint to the competent authorities, including the Public Prosecutor's Office.

**SNCGP**, November 12 notice to members

The same notice also warned against breaking the picket line. Anyone who deliberately **breaks that rule** risks losing the union's own legal support.

> In case of any deliberate violation of the right to strike by any member, the union reserves the right to withdraw legal support from that person.

**SNCGP**
