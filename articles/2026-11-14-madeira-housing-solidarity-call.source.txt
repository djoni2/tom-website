Madeira Belongs to Its People: A Call for Solidarity

A demonstration in defence of the right to housing will take place next Saturday, 14 November, in Funchal. The initiative forms part of a national mobilisation for the right to housing, taking place simultaneously in more than 30 Portuguese cities.

In Madeira, the gathering will begin this Saturday 14 November at 17:00 at Praça do Município, followed by a march to the Madeira Legislative Assembly, where the final demonstration will take place.

We call on all residents of Madeira: Madeirans, Portuguese and international residents alike to stand together in solidarity with the people of this island.

Madeira is facing an increasingly serious housing crisis, driven by institutional failures, inadequate planning and political priorities that continue to favour luxury real estate and large-scale investment over the everyday needs of local communities.

With Portugal facing one of Europe's most severe housing affordability challenges, more and more Madeirans are being priced out of their own homeland.

The public positioning of Regional Government President Miguel Albuquerque in defence of luxury real estate as an economic driver, alongside his apparent hostility towards housing protests, reflects a worrying disconnect between political priorities and the lived experiences of ordinary residents. Economic growth cannot be measured solely by investment figures, construction activity or the value of property transactions. It must also be measured by whether the people who work here can afford to live here, whether young families can build their futures here and whether local communities can remain intact.

Many people who have moved to Madeira from other countries have done so because they genuinely love this island. They value its landscapes, traditions, hospitality, culture and the warmth of its people. They have chosen to make it their home and have become part of its communities.

That is precisely why this issue must unite us.

We cannot celebrate Madeira's beauty while remaining indifferent to the difficulties faced by the people who have preserved it. We cannot enjoy its traditions, landscapes and communities while ignoring the growing risk that the very people responsible for keeping those traditions alive will no longer be able to afford to stay.

Stand with the people of Madeira. Stand for the right to live here. Stand for responsible development, affordable housing and the protection of our shared home.
