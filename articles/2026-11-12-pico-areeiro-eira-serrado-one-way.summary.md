# Road to Pico do Areeiro Turns One-Way on November 23

The forest road linking Pico do Areeiro to **Eira do Serrado** will switch to one-way traffic from November 23, the Regional Secretariat for Tourism, Environment and Culture announced. Two-way driving on the route will no longer be allowed.

## New Rule Starts on November 23, Only One Direction

From that date, drivers will only be able to travel **from Pico do Areeiro** toward Eira do Serrado. The secretariat said the change responds to a significant **rise in traffic** in the mountain area, as well as the upcoming start of construction work.

## Narrow, Winding Route Makes Crossing Vehicles Difficult

The road's **narrow profile** and winding layout make it hard for vehicles travelling in opposite directions to pass each other safely. The one-way system is meant to improve safety conditions and avoid risky situations on this stretch.

## Curral das Freiras Cable Car Project Adds to the Pressure

The secretariat linked the decision partly to the **imminent start of construction** of the Madeira SkyPark, the cable car project linking Curral das Freiras and Jardim da Serra. The project, estimated at 48.5 million euros, has faced **years of opposition** from environmental groups, citizens' movements and political parties over its impact on the valley. Combined with heavier general traffic, officials said the mountain road needed a new system to handle the load.

## Government Says Move Will Cut Collisions and Erosion Risk

According to the regional government, one-way traffic will **lower the risk** of collisions and of overtaking manoeuvres in spots with limited visibility. It should also ease pressure on the road's shoulders, reducing the risk of **erosion and rockfall**.

:::callout 💡
**Why it matters**: the secretariat also said the change will keep a clearer path open for **firefighters, ambulances and mountain rescue teams** responding to emergencies on the route.
:::

## Stops and Parking Limited to Designated Spots Only

A second change covers parking. Stopping and parking will only be allowed in **designated locations**, so that other vehicles using the road are not blocked.

The secretariat asked drivers, residents and tourism operators for their **understanding and cooperation**, and urged special attention to new road signs once the rules take effect on November 23.
