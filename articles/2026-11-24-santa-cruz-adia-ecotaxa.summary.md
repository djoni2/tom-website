# Santa Cruz Delays Plan to Double Its Tourist Tax

Santa Cruz's municipal council has **postponed for a year** any doubling of its tourist tax. The nightly charge stays at **€2 per person**, mayor Élia Ascensão confirmed, rather than rising to €4 as had been under discussion.

## Council Keeps the Nightly Tax at Two Euros

"We will postpone," Ascensão told newspaper Diário. The rise to €4 had never actually been **formally approved**, she said. It was only an option raised during an ongoing review of the municipal tax regulation.

That review began in **October**. It was meant to update rules on collection, enforcement, lodging registration and exemptions, not to set a new rate. Santa Cruz's **2027 municipal budget** does not count on any revenue from a higher tax.

## Regulation Review Continues Without a Rate Change

The council will keep reviewing the rules over the **next year** without touching the amount charged. The tax remains capped at a maximum of **seven nights** per stay, same as now.

## Mayor Cites Slowing Demand Signs for 2027

Ascensão said the decision follows warnings from **tourism operators** and booking forecasts for next year that fall short of recent results. She stressed the signals are not limited to Santa Cruz alone.

> We are sensitive to the warnings and forecasts that have been made by various agents in the sector.

**Élia Ascensão**, Mayor of Santa Cruz

Her concern follows a warning from the Commercial and Industrial Association of Funchal, known as **ACIF**. The group flagged weaker booking prospects for Christmas, New Year and summer 2027. Madeira's Regional Government has **rejected** that pessimistic outlook.

## Mayor Warns of Three Factors Behind a 'Perfect Storm'

"I believe a perfect storm is forming, one that could have catastrophic effects on the Region's main economic activity," Ascensão warned. She named **three factors**: the spread of fees, rising travel costs and international uncertainty.

> Santa Cruz does not want to be part of the problem.

**Élia Ascensão**, Mayor of Santa Cruz

## Trail Fees Are Still Set to Triple by April

Ascensão criticized the Region's wider fee policy, pointing in particular to **hiking trails**. The Regional Government has announced increases from April 2027 that push the ticket for some trails from €4.50 to **€15**.

She argued there is **no joined-up strategy** among the different bodies charging tourists, and questioned how those values are justified.

## Santa Cruz Pioneered the Charge Back in 2016

The mayor recalled that Santa Cruz was the **first municipality** in Madeira to introduce a tourist tax, despite strong political criticism at the time. The regulation was approved in 2015, and collection began in **January 2016**.

"Back then we were accused of taxes and little taxes," she recalled. "Nowadays, what is happening? Taxes, little taxes and **big taxes**."

## Annual Revenue Nears Two Million Euros

Despite the criticism, Ascensão still defends municipal taxes as a way to offset tourism's pressure on local services and territory. The issue, she said, is not the principle of charging but the **uncoordinated buildup** of costs for tourists. Santa Cruz has tried, without success, to align its charges with other municipalities.

The Ecotax brings in close to **€2 million a year** for Santa Cruz, according to Ascensão. By delaying the increase, the council gives up the chance to raise that revenue for now, choosing **sector stability** instead. A new assessment is due in a year, to decide the next step.
