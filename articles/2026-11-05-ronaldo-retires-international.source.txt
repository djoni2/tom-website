Cristiano Ronaldo has retired from international duty with Portugal; the 41-year-old earned 234 caps, scoring 146 goals; he won Euro 2016 and two Nations League titles for his country; it comes after a falling out with head coach Jorge Jesus in the last international break

Cristiano Ronaldo has retired from international football.
The 41-year-old Portugal legend has brought an end to his glittering 23-year career for his country after a record-breaking 146 goals in 234 caps .
Ronaldo took to social media to confirm the decision on Thursday morning, with the caption "Portugal will always be in me."

How 'fallout' with Jesus unfolded
Jesus took over from Roberto Martinez after the World Cup. His first games were Nations League fixtures in the September-October international break. His started Ronaldo in the first - a 1-0 win against Wales.
However, the then-Portugal captain was benched during a 2-1 win against Norway and did not come on.
Ronaldo left the national team camp on the eve of facing Denmark on October 1, sparking rumours that he was about to announce his international retirement.
Jesus denied there was a rift between the two despite reports - but a statement posted by Ronaldo on Instagram said the new manager had 'broken his promises'.

Sky Sports chief correspondent Kaveh Solhekol explained Ronaldo's statement in October, after the Portuguese forward left his international team camp
Ronaldo claimed that Jesus told him he would play 30 minutes against Norway on the day of the game only to go back on his word.
"Everyone knows the rest. I warmed up from half-time onwards, watched the minutes tick by and wasn't called on," Ronaldo said. "The coach had broken his word to me."
Ronaldo and Jesus, who used to work together at Al Nassr, subsequently held a meeting after the Norway game , which was also attended by the sporting director of the Saudi Pro League club, Simao Coutinho.
According to Ronaldo's statement, Jesus agreed to clarify the situation and say that his captain never demanded minutes and that it was a mistake to let him warm up without bringing him on.

Ronaldo says an eight-point document was drawn up to summarise what had been discussed in a subsequent meeting between Jesus, Al Nassr sporting director Coutinho and Tiago Craveiro, the player's strategic adviser.
However, Ronaldo felt the Portugal head coach's subsequent press conference bore no reflection to the conversation they had shared, which he claims ended in Jesus asking him for a hug.
He added: "It was the second time he had broken his word to me. I decided to leave the training camp without waiting for any further broken promises."

Jesus denied that any such agreement had been made with the player in a later press conference.
Despite this, Ronaldo admitted he never should have left the Portugal camp "despite the absolute breach of trust" from Jesus.
Ronaldo apologised to his team-mates and the Portuguese people over his departure from the squad and said he will accept whatever punishment is handed down to him by the Portuguese Football Federation.
That turned out to be a 30-day ban and a £215 fine .

Ronaldo's retirement statement in full
"To the Portuguese people, the day has come for me to hand back the number seven shirt to the national team. I never felt it belonged to me alone. But, for 23 years, I had the privilege and the responsibility of wearing it, defending it and carrying it with me into stadiums all over the world.
"Today, the time has come to carry it into the future. It was not an easy decision, because some decisions are not made with the mind alone. They are also made with the heart. A journey that began in August 2003 and changed my life forever has come to an end.
"When I look back at everything I experienced with the national team, I don't just see matches, goals, records or trophies. I see people. I see Portuguese people living abroad who, for ninety minutes, felt a little closer to home. I see millions of Portuguese people I have never met, but who became part of my life. Because, at the end of the day, football is so much bigger than a game. Football is about people.

"Throughout my career with the national team, I always pushed myself beyond my limits. I wanted to win. I wanted to be better. I wanted to make history. I always demanded the most of myself. I always wanted more. I always believed we could go further. And I gave everything I had for this shirt.
"There were perfect afternoons and nights. There were victories I will never forget. There were defeats that still hurt to remember. I made mistakes. I suffered. There were also moments when I couldn't give the Portuguese people what they so desperately wanted. But I never gave up. And above all, I never stopped feeling proud. To represent Portugal.
"Over the years, I learned a few very simple things. Goals fade. Trophies end up in museums. Records are eventually broken. But, there is something time can never erase: the place we hold in people's hearts. If I ever made you happy, if one of my goals ever made you embrace someone, forget a worry, or feel proud of our country, then it was all worth it.
"Because being Portuguese is also about this. We suffer together, we rise together. We believe again.
"Portugal will always need young people who dare to dream. Players who never give up. New generations to believe it is possible to go where no one has ever gone before. Because a country that keeps creating new heroes is a country that never stops believing in itself.
"To my teammates, I want to say something special. Thank you for every run, for every embrace, for every argument. For every silence before stepping onto the pitch. For every victory we celebrated together. For every defeat that made us stronger. I had the privilege of being your captain, but no captain walks alone. And you will always be a part of my story.
"To all the coaches, directors, doctors, physiotherapists, kit managers, staff members, and so many others who worked alongside me throughout these years, thank you. Many of you never appeared in the photographs. But without you, none of those photographs would have been possible.
"To my family. Thank you for sharing this dream with me. Thank you for understanding the absences, the sacrifices and the moments when the national team had to take up space in my life that also belonged to you. If I came this far, it was because we always walked hand in hand.

"And to the people of Portugal. To every Portuguese person around the world, wherever you are in the world, there is one last thing I want to tell you. There is an image I will carry with me forever. The moment our national anthem begins. I look around. I see my teammates. I see the stands. I see the children, the mothers, the fathers and the grandparents. I see Portuguese people who have travelled so far just to be there. And for a few minutes, all our differences disappear. There are no clubs, there are no colours, there are no classes, there are no professions, there are no opinions. There is only one passion: Portugal.
"That is the feeling I will carry with me for the rest of my life. Today, my journey on the pitch with the national team comes to an end. I leave with my head held high, proud of the path I have taken and knowing that I gave everything I had for Portugal. But, I will never stop being one of you. I will keep suffering, cheering and celebrating every Portuguese victory with a siuuuuu!
"I spent my life working to become the best player in the world. Today, I realise my greatest victory was earning the honour of representing the best country in the world. Thank you for these 23 years. Thank you for believing in me. Thank you for allowing me to live a dream that began when I was just a little boy in Madeira running after a football. The number 7 shirt shall remain. The pride of having worn it will be mine forever. Portugal will always be a part of me. Farewell, until we meet again."
