# Albuquerque Open to Adjusting Fees if Tourism Drops

Miguel Albuquerque, president of Madeira's Regional Government, defended the rise in fees charged to hikers on the island's trails and levadas. He was speaking during a debate in the region's **Legislative Assembly** on Tuesday.

Albuquerque told deputies he is ready to **adjust tourism policy** if visitor numbers fall next year.

## Two Opposition Deputies Challenge Fee Rises

Rafael Nunes and Gonçalo Maia Camelo, both opposition lawmakers, questioned the government's decision to raise trail fees. Albuquerque responded by tying the increase to a goal of raising the **quality of service** offered to visitors.

## President Says He Does Not Understand Sector's Concern

Albuquerque said he does not understand why tourism businesspeople are "so worried" about the **fee increases**. He argued that revenue per available room, known as RevPAR, and the sector's overall revenues have never been higher.

## Albuquerque Admits He May Adjust Policy Next Year

Albuquerque said that if tourist flows fall at the **start of next year**, his government stands ready to act. He left open the possibility of revising the tourism fees already set for 2027.

## ACIF Forecasts Up to 40% Fewer Summer Bookings

Later in the debate, Tourism Secretary **Eduardo Jesus** returned to the subject. He dismissed the business sector's warnings as "alarmist predictions."

The business association ACIF has forecast booking declines of **18% to 20%** for Christmas and New Year, and of up to **40%** for next summer.

## Secretary Says No Operator Has Reported Trouble

Jesus said that, **so far**, there is "no outlook from any operator" that could be in that situation. He challenged the business association to name the operator in difficulty.

> We wanted to know which operator was in difficulty, because we wanted to cooperate. To this day, we don't know which operator that is.
**Eduardo Jesus**, Regional Secretary for Tourism, Environment and Culture

## Some Operators Already Report a Christmas Overbooking

Jesus said the picture he sees is the **opposite** of ACIF's warning. He said some tour operators already have **overbooking** for Christmas, meaning demand now exceeds the spots available.

He did acknowledge one shift: a **new trend** toward bookings made much closer to the travel date. Jesus linked that pattern to ongoing **international instability**, rather than to the fee increases.
