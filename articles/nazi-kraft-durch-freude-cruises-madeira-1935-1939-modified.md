# German Workers Sailed to Madeira on Nazi Cruises Between 1935 and 1939

Between March 1935 and March 1939, **German workers** came to Madeira on cruises organised by the Nazi regime.

## Historical context of Nazi worker programmes in Madeira

On 9 October the historian **Irene Flunser Pimentel** described the trips on 'Prova Oral', a programme on RTP Antena 3 presented by Fernando Alvim. The regional newspaper DIÁRIO checked her account against its own archive and found it correct.

**Pimentel**, who studies twentieth-century European history, told listeners:
One of the things Hitler did was to set up a so-called welfare state. For the first time, workers went on cruises, for example, to Madeira. This was done through a leisure organisation called **Strength Through Joy**. This is often confused with the welfare state after the Second World War, but note that it was not universal. Jews, communists, socialists and so on were left out.

:::callout 💡
**Strength Through Joy**: Known in German as Kraft durch Freude (meaning "Strength Through Joy"), this Nazi regime leisure organisation was founded in 1933 inside the German Labour Front. It offered workers subsidised vacations and cruises as a tool for worker control and indoctrination. By 1939 it had sold 43 million trips, including voyages to Madeira.
:::

## Strength Through Joy origins and scale in Nazi Germany

**Kraft durch Freude** Sold 43 Million Trips Before 1939
The organisation she named, **Kraft durch Freude (KdF)**, was founded by the Nazi regime in 1933 inside the German Labour Front, the Deutsche Arbeitsfront (DAF), led by Robert Ley. The German Historical Museum calls it the most popular organisation of the regime.
German trade unions had been suppressed. KdF gave the state a way to control and indoctrinate workers at work and in their free time. By 1939 it had sold **43 million trips**, most of them one-day outings. The museum states: "Of the seven million holiday trips, **690,000** were sea voyages to Norway, Madeira or Italy".

## First Three KdF Ships Reached Funchal on 20 March

On **14 March 1935**, DIÁRIO announced the visit of the first three KdF ships:
- the 'St. Louis'
- the 'Oceana'
- 'Der Deutsche'
They would arrive on the morning of 20 March and leave on the night of the 21st. On board were "**2,600 passengers** of both sexes, from all parts of Germany, almost all workers and labourers, who come on a pleasure trip".
The visitors took a train up to **Terreiro da Luta** and came back down in the traditional carros de cesto, the island's wicker toboggans. They also went to:
- Pico do Areeiro
- Ribeira Brava
- Câmara de Lobos

## Madeira Cruise Price Analysis: 120 Reichsmarks vs 150 Reichmark Wage
What passengers paid is less clear. The 1935 report said the sum was small: "These workers pay a **minimal amount** for their trip, which allows them to make it, since otherwise it would not be possible for them."
**1935 report** stated workers paid a minimal amount for their trip. The German Historical Museum gives a harder figure. "Prices ranged from one to five Reichsmarks for short trips and 120 Reichsmarks for a cruise to Madeira, which was practically out of reach for a worker with a monthly wage of about 150 Reichsmarks," it states. Neither source explains the other.

## Political symbolism during Madeira visits in October 1938

**Robert Ley** Praised Hitler at a Reception on the St. Louis
The political purpose was not hidden. **Ley**, head of the DAF, sailed on that first trip and was received by the civil governor of Madeira. He gave a reception for local authorities aboard the 'St. Louis', speaking "with great enthusiasm" as DIÁRIO put it. He called the visit "a great success of the ideas of the new Germany, advocated by the führer Adolf Hitler".



Bands Played **Both Anthems** as Two Ships Anchored in October 1938
The cruises called at Lisbon on the way and became popular among German workers. The KdF fleet grew and was modernised. On 19 October 1938, DIÁRIO reported the arrival in Funchal, the day before, of the 'Wilhelm Gustloff' and the 'Stuttgart'. **DIÁRIO described** the arrival.
As they anchored in the port, the **on-board bands** played the German and Portuguese national anthems. The excursionists joined in chorus, amplified by loudspeakers that could be clearly heard on land. It added that some rockets went up and, as they burst, dropped small Portuguese and German flags. The excursions then left for Terreiro da Luta and Santo da Serra.

## Portuguese Reception and Media Coverage of KdF Visitors

Between 1935 and 1939, **Portuguese newspapers** documented the arrival of thousands of German workers with notable attention.
"**It is pleasing** for us to record that Portugal is the first country to receive this embassy of peace and work" - O Comércio do Porto, 20 March 1935
"exemplary '**order, discipline and joy**'" - O Século, 17 March 1935

The **Diário Português** (Rio de Janeiro) described visitors on 23 October 1937.
"**fat ladies**, strong men, non-idle workers" - Diário Português (Rio de Janeiro), 23 October 1937
**António de Menezes** added.
"They visit the city in dense groups, they move **cafés and breweries**." - António de Menezes

**António de Menezes** continued.
"Since Germany is the country that annually sends **highest number of tourists** to Portugal." - António de Menezes
**António de Menezes** added.
"Nowadays he dedicates himself more to the pleasure of travel and considers the KdF our **best propaganda agent**." - António de Menezes

**Returning workers** carried memories of their time in Portugal. When going back to Germany, these workers recalled their experiences.
"full of **warmth of the southern** climate." - Returning workers
"vibrant with **welcome of our people** and impregnated with the balm of a 15-day journey in the blue sphere of the Atlantic." - Returning workers

## Academic research on the Madeira visits since 1995

The **University of Madeira Researchers** Have Studied the Trips Since 1995
The University of Madeira (UMa) has studied these visits since 1995. **July 1995**, Professor Martina Emonts presented a paper on the National Socialist myth of the island at the university's colloquium on travel writing. She worked from four travel accounts that defend Nazism and point to similarities between the regimes led by Hitler and Salazar.
One detail was constant across the trips. "**A constant** in the trips to Madeira is the playing of the national anthems," Emonts said. "The Portuguese anthem is saluted in the Nazi manner, with the arm raised."


In **September 2011**, Emonts and Paulo Miguel Rodrigues supervised Helena Perneta's dissertation on Madeira and the Germans between 1917 and 1939, which gives the cruises a chapter of their own.
The visits stopped in **1939**, shortly before the Second World War began, when Germany moved its workers and its ships to the war effort.
