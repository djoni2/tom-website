# Survivor Recalls Deadly 1977 TAP Plane Crash in Madeira

A TAP Boeing 727-200 arriving from Lisbon overran the runway at Madeira's old airport on **19 November 1977**. It was the plane's third landing attempt.

Portuguese broadcaster RTP covered the 49th anniversary with an interview with Manuel Torres, one of the crash's **33 survivors**.

## Third Landing Attempt Overran Santa Catarina's Short Runway

The Boeing came down well beyond the designated landing zone at the then **Santa Catarina Airport**, Madeira's airport at the time. With no room left to stop, it ran off the end of the runway and broke apart.

## 131 of 164 Aboard Died in the Crash

Of the **164 people** aboard, 131 died. Torres was one of the 33 people who survived.

## Torres, 17 at the Time, Saved a Child but Lost His Mother

Torres was **17 years old** when the plane crashed. That night, he saved a child, but his own mother died.

He told RTP that the memory has **never left him**. Still, he says it has not ruined his life: he describes himself as always fine, always the same person.

When he fell into the sea, Torres **hit his head** on the plane's wing and was cut. He swam to shore.

He then went back into the water to help another survivor. He pulled the person out and gave them **mouth-to-mouth resuscitation**, without stopping until they began to revive.

He found a man to **carry the survivor** to the hospital.

He then found his own mother. He carried her to the shore and realized **she was dead**. With no strength left, he had to ask others to get him to the hospital too.

## Only 5 of the 33 Survivors Are Still Alive

By Torres's count, only **5 of the 33** survivors are still alive, 49 years after the crash.

Every year, Torres returns to **Santa Cruz**, the municipality where the airport sits, on the anniversary. He said he once traveled on the exact date itself.

Each time, he **lays flowers** in the sea as a small tribute. He says it does not matter if they disappear by the next day.

## Runway Later Grew to Its Current 2,781 Meters

The crash ended up **shaping the airport's future**, RTP reported. The runway was extended to 1,800 meters in the 1980s.

A further expansion in 2000 stretched it to its current **2,781 meters**.

The old, short runway at Santa Catarina has long since been replaced, but concerns about Madeira's single airport have not disappeared. The Portuguese Communist Party recently asked the government to study a backup airport for the island.
