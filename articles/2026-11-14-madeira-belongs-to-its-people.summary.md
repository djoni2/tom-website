# Madeira Belongs to Its People: A Call for Solidarity

A demonstration for the right to housing takes place **today in Funchal**. It is part of a national mobilization held at the same time in more than 30 Portuguese cities.

We call on everyone who lives on this island, Madeirans, other Portuguese and international residents alike. **Stand together** in solidarity with the people of Madeira.

## March Begins at 17:00 in Praça do Município Today

The gathering starts at **17:00** in Praça do Município, Funchal's main square. From there, protesters march to the Assembleia Legislativa da Madeira, the regional parliament, where the final rally takes place.

**[Times of Madeira reported](https://www.timesofmadeira.com/madeira-housing-protest-funchal)** the march's route and lineup on November 8. Today's demonstration follows a similar **cost-of-living protest** held in Funchal last month.

## More Than 30 Portuguese Cities Join Today's Mobilization

Today's march is not an isolated event. It is one part of a **nationwide mobilization** for the right to housing. Demonstrations are held at the same time in more than 30 cities across Portugal.

Madeira is facing an increasingly serious housing crisis. In our view, it is driven by **institutional failures**, inadequate planning and political priorities. Those priorities keep favouring luxury real estate and large-scale investment over the everyday needs of local communities.

Portugal faces one of Europe's most severe housing affordability challenges. More and more Madeirans are being **priced out** of their own homeland.

## Portugal's House Price-to-Income Ratio Rose 60.8% Since 2015

That affordability problem shows up clearly in the numbers. According to Eurostat, the ratio of house prices to household income across the EU was **6.8% higher** in 2025 than in 2015.

Portugal recorded the **largest increase** of any EU country over that decade, at 60.8%. The Netherlands followed at 34.3%, then Hungary at 26.5%, Czechia at 25.8% and Spain at 24.5%. Romania moved the opposite way, posting the largest decrease, at -42.8%, followed by Finland at -25.2% and Bulgaria at -16.2%.

## Editorial Calls Out Albuquerque's Defence of Luxury Projects

Regional Government President **Miguel Albuquerque** has publicly defended luxury real estate investment as a driver of the regional economy. He has also shown little patience for housing protests. In our view, that stance exposes a troubling gap between political priorities and what ordinary residents are living through.

Economic growth cannot be measured only by investment figures, construction activity or property values, in our view. It should also be measured by whether the **people who work here** can afford to live here. And whether young families can build their futures here, and local communities can stay intact.

**[A separate report](https://www.timesofmadeira.com/can-a-court-lift-president-albuquerques-immunity)** examined the legal questions surrounding Albuquerque's position.

## Call for Solidarity Reaches Madeira's International Residents

Many people who have moved to Madeira from other countries did so because they **genuinely love** this island. They value its landscapes, traditions, hospitality, culture and the warmth of its people. They have chosen to make it their home and have become part of its communities.

That is precisely why this issue must **unite us**. We cannot celebrate Madeira's beauty while remaining indifferent to the difficulties faced by the people who have preserved it.

We cannot enjoy its traditions, landscapes and communities while ignoring a growing risk. The very people who keep those traditions alive may no longer be able to **afford to stay**.

Stand with the people of Madeira today. Stand for the **right to live here**. Stand for responsible development, affordable housing and the protection of our shared home.
