# IPMA Rules Out Comparison Between Storm and 2010 Disaster

Madeira is bracing for a stormy night, but weather officials say it will not repeat the island's worst disaster. **Ricardo Tavares** is the regional delegate of Portugal's weather institute, IPMA. He said conditions expected Tuesday night and into Wednesday morning are not comparable to the storm of **February 20, 2010**.

He made the point at a **press conference on Tuesday**, at the headquarters of the Regional Civil Protection Service. He laid out the differences between the two events, above all in accumulated rainfall.

## IPMA Forecasts 130 to 250 Millimeters of Rain

The storm now approaching is linked to **depression Charles**. IPMA expects accumulated rainfall of **130 to 250 millimeters**, equivalent to the same range in liters per square meter. Totals will vary significantly between different parts of the island.

## Highlands Logged 700 Millimeters Before 2010 Tragedy

Tavares recalled that the 2010 storm followed more than a month of intense rain, with several days of very heavy accumulation. In the period before that tragedy, the island's highlands had gathered around **700 millimeters** of rain, while totals in Funchal passed **300 millimeters**.

> It isn't exactly related to this situation.

**Ricardo Tavares**, regional delegate of IPMA

## Winds May Top 140 Km/h in the Mountains

The most critical period runs from **21:00 to 06:00**, with strong, persistent rain focused on the south coast and the highlands. Wind gusts could reach 100 km/h generally, and **over 140 km/h** in the mountains, where conditions peak between midnight and 06:00.

From the start of Wednesday morning, IPMA expects wind and rain to **gradually ease**.
