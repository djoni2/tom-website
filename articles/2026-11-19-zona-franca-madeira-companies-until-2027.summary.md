# Parliament Extends Madeira Free Zone Entry to 2027

Portugal's parliament has **extended by one year** the deadline for new companies to join the Madeira Free Trade Zone (ZFM). Companies that get licensed by the **end of 2027** will still qualify for a special 5% corporate tax rate, running until 2033.

## PS Amendment Passes During November 19 Budget Vote

The change was approved today as part of the detailed vote on Portugal's **2027 State Budget** (OE2027). It was proposed by the parliamentary group of the **Socialist Party (PS)**.

Without the amendment, only companies already licensed to operate in the ZFM by the end of 2026 would have kept the tax benefits. Those benefits are reserved for authorized entities of the **International Business Centre** of Madeira (CINM). The new rule pushes that licensing window out by a full year.

## Special Tax Rate of 5% Holds Firm Until 2033

A year ago, the **2026 State Budget** had already extended the zone's tax rules. Companies licensed by a set date would keep the special 5% corporate tax rate until **2033**. At the time, that cutoff date was the end of 2026.

Today's PS amendment **moves that cutoff** to the end of 2027, without changing the 2033 date. A company licensed next year locks in the same reduced rate as one licensed this year, for the same length of time.

## JPP's Bid to Push Deadline to 2028 Rejected

Lawmakers also voted down a competing proposal from the **JPP** party. It would have extended the company entry window further, to 31 December **2028**, and required the government to report back to parliament.

Under JPP's proposal, the government would have had until **September 30, 2027**, to report back to the Assembleia da República. That report was meant to be an "evaluation of the execution of the extension" of the ZFM regime. It would have had to cover the estimated tax cost, investment, jobs and the economic activity actually generated in Madeira.

**Parliament rejected** the proposal.

## Tax Break Tops Out at €205.5 Million for Biggest Employers

The reduced 5% rate does not apply without limit. It only covers taxable profit up to a cap that **rises with the jobs** a company creates in the region.

To qualify at all, a company has to **create and keep jobs** in the region. The reduced rate only applies to profit from activities actually carried out in Madeira.

## Three Areas Make Up the Free Trade Zone

The ZFM is split into **three separate areas**: international services, the industrial free zone, and the international ship registry, known locally as "MAR".

A company first needs authorization from the **Sociedade de Desenvolvimento** da Madeira (SDM). That regional public company keeps the registry of licensed entities up to date. Only then can a company operate in any of the three areas and claim the incentives.

## Madeira's Rate Undercuts Portugal's 20% Mainland Tax

Madeira can offer companies a corporate tax rate far below the mainland's **standard 20% rate**, because of European Union law. The Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union lets "outermost regions" benefit from regional development aid. Madeira, the Azores, the Canary Islands and French Guiana all qualify.

In Madeira, that mechanism takes the form of **tax-based operating aid**: the ZFM itself. It is meant to offset the structural disadvantages businesses face from operating in an isolated region. Because companies in the zone receive this special aid, they must meet conditions that avoid distorting EU competition.

## Brussels Ruled Aid Illegal in 2020

The ZFM's compliance with EU rules has been tested before. In **2020**, after an investigation opened in 2018, the European Commission concluded that Portugal had applied the aid regime illegally. It said Portugal had not followed the EU's own state aid decisions from 2007 and 2013.
