# Madeira's Home-Buyer Grants Stuck Without Key Regulation

Madeira's Regional Government created a housing program called Casa Própria more than **three years ago**, promising cheaper homes and direct financial support for buyers. The plan has not produced those effects yet, according to official documents reviewed by the Portuguese newspaper DIÁRIO.

During a parliamentary debate on **November 17**, JPP deputy Lina Pereira accused the government of leaving the program shelved since 2023. She said it still lacks the regulation needed to put it into practice.

The PSD's Vera Duarte rejected the claim that the program had been abandoned. Duarte blamed the delays on **political instability** in the region and pointed to ongoing work to evaluate and adjust the model.

DIÁRIO **checked the official record** to see what actually happened.

## A 2023 Decree Promised Grants Covering 7.5% of a Home's Price

Casa Própria was created by **Decreto Legislativo Regional** n.º 25/2023/M, published on July 14, 2023. It aimed to make homebuying easier through the construction and renovation of properties priced below the open market.

The model relied on private developers and cooperatives, with incentives to build affordable homes. It promised buyers non-repayable grants of **up to 7.5%** of a home's price toward the down payment.

The government said at launch it expected to build an average of **200 homes a year**, for a total of 2,000 over a decade.

## Articles 34 and 35 Made Support Conditional on a Missing Order

Passing the decree was not enough to make the program work. **Article 34** required the application rules to be set by a joint order, known as a portaria. That order had to come from the government members responsible for finance and housing.

Article 35 went further: the **promised support** would only take effect once that order entered into force. More than three years after the decree, that order still has not been published.

## Government Committed Over €1.2 Million to Land by 2024

The documents show Casa Própria was not entirely frozen. In **December 2023**, the government authorized spending of up to €480,000 to buy land. In 2024, it approved another authorization, up to €740,000, for the same purpose.

A 2024 report from Investimentos Habitacionais da Madeira (IHM), the public body managing the program, confirms the purchase. The plot sits in **Penteada, Funchal**, and is tied to the project.

## Caminho dos Saltos Plot, Over 5,580 Square Meters, Left in 2025

In September 2023, the government assigned Casa Própria two plots: one in Penteada, one in Caminho dos Saltos. In **July 2025**, it pulled the Caminho dos Saltos plot out of the program.

That plot measured roughly **5,580 square meters**. The government redirected it to a different affordable-housing mechanism instead.

During the debate, PS deputy Victor Freitas questioned Pereira about the resolution, arguing that the homes Madeira's government is currently building are funded by **EU and state-budget money**. That money was approved under the previous national government, led by António Costa, not through Casa Própria.

## Promised Order Still Missing 16 Months After Government's Pledge

The clearest sign of where things stand comes from the Madeira government's own official gazette. In a resolution published on **July 4, 2025**, the government said the order needed to apply Casa Própria was being finalized. More than 16 months have passed since.

IHM's own website still tells interested families that application periods will be announced only after that regulatory order is published. DIÁRIO found **no record** of any application window opening, or of any support contract signed under the 2023 law.

## 2026 Budget Sets Aside €500,000 as Past Spending Lags

Madeira's PIDDAR investment plan for 2026 lists about €1.19 million in accumulated spending on Casa Própria from previous years. It adds a further **€500,000 budgeted** for this year. That spending covers land and preparatory work, not the grants the law promised to families.

There is a difference between **investing in preparation** and actually delivering the benefits that justified creating a program. On the documents DIÁRIO reviewed, Casa Própria has had the first without the second.

JPP's claim holds up against the record. Casa Própria was created in **July 2023**. More than three years later, it still lacks the regulation its own law requires before any support can reach a buyer.

IHM itself keeps **applications on hold** until that order is published. The government said in July 2025 it was close to finishing it.

It would be inaccurate to say nothing happened: land was bought, money was authorized, and preparatory work went ahead. But none of that amounts to the program actually **being open** to the families it was built for.
