# The School That Came Before Madeira's University

More than **150 years** before the University of Madeira was created in 1988, the island already had an institution of higher education. The Escola Médico-Cirúrgica, or Medical-Surgical School, trained doctors in Funchal for 73 years before it was shut down.

## Decree Signed on 29 December 1836

The Portuguese government created the school by decree on **29 December 1836**. The aim was to address a shortage of doctors on the island. It was installed in the **Hospital de Santa Isabel**, in the building where the Regional Government Palace now stands. Part of that hospital complex was later demolished in the 1930s to make way for Avenida Zarco.

## Classes Began in October 1837

The school started functioning in **October 1837**. A solemn opening ceremony followed about a year later, on 15 October 1838. Entry requirements were basic at first: applicants only needed to read, write and count to enroll. The course initially lasted **three years**.

## Eleven Students Enrolled in the First Year

In its first year, **11 students** registered at the school. Five of them completed the course in 1840, becoming the **first doctors** trained at the institution.

## About 240 Doctors Trained Over 73 Years

Over its **73 years** of existence, the Escola Médico-Cirúrgica trained roughly **240 doctors**. The institution contributed to the development of teaching and medical care across the archipelago. It was shut down in November 1910.

## University of Madeira Founded in 1988

The Universidade da Madeira was only founded in **1988**, more than seven decades after the Escola Médico-Cirúrgica closed. The 19th-century school is remembered today as the island's **first institution** of higher education, long before the modern university took on that role.
