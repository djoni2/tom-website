# Laranjada: Madeira's Orange Soda Is Older Than Coca-Cola

Ask for a Laranjada in any bar in Madeira and you get a piece of history. The **bright orange soda** first appeared in Funchal in 1872, when Portugal was still a monarchy. That makes it 14 years older than Coca-Cola.

Laranjada was the first soft drink produced **in Portugal**, according to its maker, the Empresa de Cervejas da Madeira (ECM). It is sold in bars, restaurants and supermarkets across the archipelago. It is still one of the most popular drinks in the region.

## Henry Price Miles, Who Arrived From Wales in 1856, Created It

The drink is credited to **Henry Price Miles**. He left Wales with his mother as a child and arrived in Madeira in 1856. From the age of 12, he worked as an apprentice for the wine merchant James Rutherford.

In 1872, Miles bought the Rutherfords' wine company and founded the **Atlantic Brewery** (Cervejaria Atlântica) in Funchal. According to the regional government's Madeira Circular programme, the business **began with Laranjada**, its first soft drink.

His wine business also survives today, as the **Miles Madeira** wine brand. The Miles family kept a stake in the drink's maker for more than a century, until 2004.

## A Strong Orange Peel Taste and 42 Calories per 100 ml

ECM describes Laranjada as **strongly carbonated**, clear and slightly sour. It has a bright orange colour and a strong smell and taste of **orange peel**. The company calls it a soft drink made from natural orange extract.

The recipe is short: water, sugar, carbon dioxide, citric acid, flavourings and two colourings, **E102 and E122**. Because of these colourings, the label warns that the drink may have a negative effect on activity and attention in children.

ECM says the drink should be **served well chilled** and kept away from sunlight and strong smells. The English version of the label also says to shake it before opening. According to the company, it has kept its **characteristic flavour** to this day.

## Mixed With Wine or Coral at Festivals Across Madeira's Villages

Laranjada has always been tied to **the island's festivals**. According to ECM, there is no traditional party without a "Laranjada cocktail". This is a mix of the soda with **wine or Coral beer**, the island's lager.

At an arraial, a village street festival, someone is almost always drinking **wine with Laranjada** next to their espetada, according to Visit Madeira. The soda softens the alcohol in the wine. ECM also recommends it for a sangria.

Visit Madeira, the regional tourism board, calls Laranjada one of the **oldest soft drinks** in the world. It says the mix with wine or beer keeps alive a tradition that is part of Madeiran culture.

## A Second Funchal Factory Made Laranjada From 1922

For a time, the drink had **two homes**. In 1922, the Araújo and Tavares families opened the **Fábrica Leão** in Funchal. Its best-known products were a Pilsner beer and the famous orange soda Laranjada.

A bank collapse and a deep recession followed in the late 1920s and early 1930s. Both companies decided that **Madeira was too small** for two breweries. In 1934 they merged into ECM, which has made Laranjada ever since.

Laranjada later got a famous younger sibling. Created in 1970, **Brisa Maracujá** was the world's first soft drink made with pure passion fruit juice, according to Visit Madeira.

## Ahead of Coca-Cola in Madeira During a 26-Year Licence

Coca-Cola reached Portugal only in 1977, after decades in which the **[Estado Novo](https://www.timesofmadeira.com/antonio-salazars-estado-novo)** dictatorship kept it out. By then, Laranjada had been on sale in Madeira for **105 years**.

From 1977 to 2003, ECM bottled and sold Coca-Cola in Madeira. According to the history of ECM on Portuguese Wikipedia, the American drink sold less than the brewery's **own brands**. It says this was almost unheard of around the world.

In Madeira, Laranjada still has a **bigger market share** than Coca-Cola, the same page says. No sales figures are given.

## Sold in Bottles From 0.5 Litres to a 20-Litre Bar Tank

Laranjada comes in **returnable and non-returnable** packs. Bars can also buy it in a 20-litre tank.

All of ECM's glass bottles made and sold in Madeira are **returnable**, according to the regional government's Madeira Circular programme. About 90% of the bottles come back, and each one is used **36 times** on average.

## One Grand Gold Medal and a 2023 Campaign About Childhood

According to ECM's product page, Laranjada has won **four Monde Selection medals**: one Grand Gold, two silver and one bronze. Monde Selection awards are not a competition, though: only products that **pay to enter** are judged.

In August 2023, the Madeiran agency **Urbanistas** took over the brand's digital marketing. Its first campaign had the slogan "Everything changes, except the taste of Laranjada".

> "Laranjada is part of the childhood memories of every Madeiran, and we wanted to bring those memories to the surface."

**Urbanistas**, advertising agency, in a press release

The agency said the **first reaction** could not have been better, with followers of the brand sharing stories from their childhood.

## Pestana Group Has Owned the Maker of Laranjada Since 2004

The hotel company **Pestana Group** entered ECM in 1994 and bought out the Miles family's last shares in 2004. In 1996, ECM opened a new factory at the **Socorridos industrial park**.

In 2022, the brewery celebrated **150 years** of production in Madeira. At the time, ECM had about 250 employees and served around 3,500 customers.
