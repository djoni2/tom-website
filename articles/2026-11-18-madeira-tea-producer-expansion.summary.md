# Madeira's Only Tea Grower Plans to Double His Plantation

Paulo Rodrigues is **Madeira's only tea grower**. He wants to double the size of his plantation at Santo da Serra, taking it from half a hectare to a full hectare.

Rodrigues, an entrepreneur and agronomist, grows **Camellia sinensis**, the plant that all tea comes from. He grows it in two varieties: the Chinese type and the Indian Assamica.

## A Project That Began on Half a Hectare in 2018

Rodrigues started the project in **2018**. Today, half a hectare is already planted and producing tea, and he wants to double that area.

> The plantation started, the whole work started in 2018. Today we have half a hectare already planted and in production, and we intend to increase it to double.

**Paulo Rodrigues**, entrepreneur and agronomist

He is already **reinforcing the land** to reach a full hectare. The plants are doing well, he said, and he expects the project to become another asset for the region's agriculture.

## Annual Harvest Brings In About Two Tonnes of Tea

Annual production currently runs at **about two tonnes**, picked across several harvests over the year. Once the leaves are dried, output per hectare should land between 1.5 and two tonnes. The plantation itself can keep producing for **30 to 40 years**.

Rodrigues frames the output as **an annual figure**. In practice, picking happens several times a year, whenever the plants have grown enough, and his number is the total once those harvests are added up.

## Branded 1419 Tea Also Comes Infused With Local Fruit

The company's registered brand is called **1419 tea**. Several of its products add regional fruit to the tea itself.

> Our registered brand is called 1419, 1419 tea. We already have several teas under this brand with regional fruits added, from strawberry to pitanga and other fruits.

**Paulo Rodrigues**, entrepreneur and agronomist

Pitanga, also known as the Surinam cherry, is one of several **regional fruits** used. Rodrigues said the fruit adds its own aroma and flavor on top of the tea's own.

**Did you know?**: 1419 is also the year Portuguese navigators are traditionally said to have reached Madeira, more than two centuries before anyone grew tea on the island.

## Commercial Tea Growing on Madeira Dates to the 1800s

Tea growing on Madeira is not new. The practice goes back to the **19th century**, when the British consul Henry Veitch ran early tea plantings at **Jardim da Serra**.

More than two centuries later, Rodrigues is trying to revive that tradition at Santo da Serra. He uses the same **Camellia sinensis** plant, in its Chinese and Assamica varieties.
