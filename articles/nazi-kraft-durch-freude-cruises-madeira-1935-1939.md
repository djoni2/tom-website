# German Workers Sailed to Madeira on Nazi Cruises Between 1935 and 1939

Between March 1935 and March 1939, **German workers** came to Madeira on cruises organised by the Nazi regime.

## Strength Through Joy Offered 43 Million Leisure Trips by 1939

One of the things **[Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler)** did was to set up a so-called welfare state. For the first time, workers went on cruises, for example, to Madeira. This was done through a leisure organisation called Strength Through Joy. The organisation she named, **Kraft durch Freude (KdF)**, was founded by the Nazi regime in 1933 inside the German Labour Front, the Deutsche Arbeitsfront (DAF), led by **[Robert Ley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Ley)**. The German Historical Museum calls it the most popular organisation of the regime.

By 1939 it had sold **43 million trips**, most of them one-day outings. "Of the seven million holiday trips, 690,000 were sea voyages to Norway, Madeira or Italy," the museum states.

## First Three KdF Ships Reached Funchal on 20 March 1935

On **14 March 1935**, DIÁRIO announced the visit of the first three KdF ships: the 'St. Louis', the 'Oceana', and 'Der Deutsche'. They would arrive on the morning of 20 March and leave on the night of the 21st. On board were "2,600 passengers of both sexes, from all parts of Germany, almost all workers and labourers, who come on a pleasure trip".

:::callout 💡
**Important**: The visitors took a train up to Terreiro da Luta and came back down in the traditional carros de cesto, the island's wicker toboggans. They also went to Pico do Areeiro, Ribeira Brava and Câmara de Lobos.
:::

## Madeira Cruises Pricing Revealed Conflicting Reports

What passengers paid is less clear. The 1935 report said the sum was small: "These workers pay a **minimal amount** for their trip, which allows them to make it, since otherwise it would not be possible for them."

**1935 report** stated workers paid a minimal amount for their trip. The German Historical Museum gives a harder figure. "Prices ranged from one to five units for short trips and 120 units for a cruise to Madeira, which was practically out of reach for a worker with a monthly wage of about 150 units," it states. Neither source explains the other.

## Political Symbolism Was Evident in Anthem Playing and Flag Displays in October 1938

The political purpose was not hidden. **Ley**, head of the DAF, sailed on that first trip and was received by the civil governor of Madeira. He gave a reception for local authorities aboard the 'St. Louis', speaking "with great enthusiasm" as DIÁRIO put it. He called the visit "a great success of the ideas of the new Germany, advocated by the führer Adolf Hitler".

On 19 October 1938, DIÁRIO reported the arrival in Funchal, the day before, of the 'Wilhelm Gustloff' and the 'Stuttgart'. **DIÁRIO described** the arrival. "As they anchored in the port the on-board bands played the German and Portuguese national anthems, joined in chorus by the excursionists, who, amplified by loudspeakers, could be clearly heard on land." It added that "some rockets went up and, as they burst, dropped small Portuguese and German flags". The excursions then left for Terreiro da Luta and Santo da Serra.

## University of Madeira Researchers Have Studied the Trips Since 1995

The University of Madeira (UMa) has studied these visits since 1995. **July 1995**, Professor **[Irene Flunser Pimentel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Flunser_Pimentel)** presented a paper on the National Socialist myth of the island at the university's colloquium on travel writing. She worked from four travel accounts that defend Nazism and point to similarities between the regimes led by Hitler and Salazar.

In **September 2011**, Emonts and Paulo Miguel Rodrigues supervised Helena Perneta's dissertation on Madeira and the Germans between 1917 and 1939, which gives the cruises a chapter of their own.

The visits stopped in **1939**, shortly before the Second World War began, when Germany moved its workers and its ships to the war effort.
